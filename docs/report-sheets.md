# Report Sheets — Beginner Guide

**Education only. Never medical advice.** This page explains report sheet basics for learning purposes. Always follow your facility's policy. If anything here conflicts with your facility's policy, your facility's policy governs.

## What a report sheet is

A report sheet is a working document you carry during your shift to keep track of your patients. It is your personal reference, not the official medical record. It helps you remember tasks, times, and key facts between handoffs, rounds, and med passes.

## What typically goes on a report sheet

Contents vary by unit and by preference. Common elements:

- Patient name/initials, room and bed (follow your facility's identifier policy)
- Diagnosis and reason for admission
- Code status
- Allergies and reaction type
- Isolation precautions
- Fall risk and mobility level
- Diet and swallowing precautions
- IV access, lines, drains, devices
- Scheduled medications and times
- PRN medications given (time, dose, response)
- Vital signs and trends
- Pending tests/procedures/consults with times
- Pending provider callbacks/results
- Wound care, dressings, drain output
- Intake/output estimates
- Tasks to do before shift end
- Handoff notes for the next shift

## Tips for using a report sheet

- **Keep it updated.** Write things down as they happen rather than waiting until the end of the shift.
- **Use abbreviations your facility approves.** Avoid ambiguous or "do-not-use" abbreviations per policy.
- **Prioritize.** Mark time-critical items (meds due, procedures pending, labs to follow up).
- **Carry it securely.** Keep it on your person or in a designated secure location; never leave it in public areas.
- **Use one sheet per patient** or one sheet per shift, whatever your unit prefers — consistency matters more than format.
- **Cross-check with the MAR and flowsheets.** Your sheet is a memory aid, not a source of truth.
- **Review at handoff.** Use your sheet to give and receive report, then reconcile any differences.

## Privacy reminders

A report sheet contains patient information. Treat it like part of the medical record:

- Keep it out of public view.
- Do not leave it at a workstation, in a break room, or in a pocket you leave unattended.
- Shred or place in secure disposal bins when the shift ends or the sheet is full — per facility policy.
- Never photograph a report sheet and send it outside approved channels.

## AI and report sheets (education only)

AI can help you brainstorm what categories to include on a personal report sheet or rewrite a practice entry in clearer language using fictional cases. Never paste real patient information into an AI tool. AI cannot access your facility's EHR and cannot tell you what is true about a real patient. Your clinical judgment and your facility's forms govern.
