# Shift Recovery — Beginner Guide

**Education only. Never medical advice.** This page explains shift-recovery basics for learning purposes. Always follow your facility's policy. If anything here conflicts with your facility's policy, your facility's policy governs.

## What shift recovery is

Shift recovery is what you do after a shift — and between shifts — to restore your energy, process the day, and prepare for the next one. Healthcare work is demanding, and recovery is part of doing the job safely over the long term.

## Right after your shift

- **Debrief if you need to.** Talk through anything difficult with a trusted colleague, supervisor, or employee assistance program, keeping patient details private.
- **Hydrate and eat.** Even a small snack and water help your body recover.
- **Change out of scrubs/work clothes** per facility policy; store or launder them as required.
- **Wash your hands** and any exposed skin.
- **Create a mental "stop" point.** Write down anything still on your mind (tasks, worries, questions for the next shift) so it doesn't follow you home. A simple "brain dump" list works well.

## Between shifts (sleep and rest)

- Protect your sleep schedule, especially after night shifts. Dark room, cool temperature, limited screens before bed.
- Use caffeine strategically — early in your shift, not near bedtime.
- If you rotate shifts, try to rotate forward (days → evenings → nights) when you have a choice; it's easier on the body.
- Take your days off. Recovery accumulates over time.

## Processing hard moments

Healthcare shifts can include deaths, errors, conflicts, or stressful situations. It's normal to feel affected.

- Acknowledge the feeling rather than pushing it away.
- Use your facility's support resources: employee assistance program (EAP), chaplain, peer support, or manager.
- If feelings are intense, persistent, or interfering with daily life, reach out to a qualified mental health professional or your healthcare provider.
- In a crisis, call or text 988 (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, U.S.) or your local emergency number.

## Error or near-miss after a shift

If you realize after a shift that something may have gone wrong:

- Follow your facility's incident reporting policy promptly.
- Report to your charge nurse or manager per policy.
- Do not alter the medical record. Document facts only.
- Seek support — being involved in an error is stressful, and help is available.

## Staying sustainable

- Know your limits. Regularly skipping meals, breaks, or sleep leads to burnout and errors.
- Use your paid time off.
- Keep hobbies and connections outside of work.
- Ask for help early — from your manager, EAP, or colleagues.

## AI and shift recovery (education only)

AI can help you draft a simple after-shift reflection template or suggest low-energy recovery routines using fictional examples. Never paste patient details, coworker names, or shift schedules into an AI tool. AI cannot replace professional mental health support. If you are struggling, talk to a qualified professional.
