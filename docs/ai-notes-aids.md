# AI Aids for Drafting Notes — Education Only

**Education only. Never medical advice.** This page describes how AI can be used as a drafting aid for notes, with strict limits. Always follow your facility's policy. If anything here conflicts with your facility's policy, your facility's policy governs.

## The golden rules

1. **Never paste real patient information into an AI tool.** No names, dates of birth, medical record numbers, room numbers, dates of service, or any detail that could identify a person. Assume anything you type into a public AI tool could be seen by others.
2. **AI cannot access your EHR.** It does not know your patient's real vitals, meds, or history. Anything it writes is a guess until you verify it.
3. **AI output is a draft, not a note.** A qualified clinician must review, edit, and sign off on anything that goes into the medical record. You remain responsible for everything you document.
4. **Follow your facility's policy.** Some facilities prohibit AI for documentation entirely. Others allow it with limits. Know your rule before you use it.

## What AI can help with (education only, using fictional practice cases)

- Rewording a practice note for clarity and tone
- Organizing a note into a standard format (e.g., SOAP: Subjective, Objective, Assessment, Plan)
- Drafting patient-education handout language (to be reviewed by a clinician before giving to a patient)
- Creating practice scenarios to learn documentation structure
- Explaining documentation terms in plain language

## What AI should NOT do

- Determine a diagnosis, treatment, or plan for a real patient
- Replace clinical assessment or judgment
- Generate a note you copy-paste directly into a real chart without review
- Handle real patient data of any kind

## Example: fictional practice only

Practice case (completely made up):

> Subjective: "Practice patient" reports feeling better today, less shortness of breath.
> Objective: Practice vitals: T 98.6, HR 88, RR 18, SpO2 96% on room air. Practice lung sounds clear.
> Assessment: Practice — improving pneumonia.
> Plan: Practice — continue current meds, follow up in the morning.

AI rewrite aid (fictional):

> The practice patient reports improved dyspnea today. Practice vital signs are stable: T 98.6°F, HR 88, RR 18, SpO2 96% on room air. Practice lung sounds are clear to auscultation. Assessment: improving community-acquired pneumonia. Plan: continue current treatment plan; reassess in the morning.

**None of this is about a real person. Do not document real patients this way.**

## Before you document anything in a real chart

- Verify every fact against the patient's actual record, your own assessment, and your facility's forms.
- Confirm your facility permits AI-assisted drafting.
- Sign or authenticate the note per your credentialing and facility policy.
- Remember: the name on the note is accountable for the note.

## Privacy quick check

Before typing anything into an AI tool, ask:

- Could this identify a real patient? If yes, stop.
- Is this my facility's approved tool? If unsure, don't use it.
- Am I copying this into a real chart? If yes, a clinician must review it first.

When in doubt, leave it out. Documentation you write yourself, based on your own assessment, is always the safest choice.
