Shift-Prep Checklist

Education only. Never medical advice. This checklist helps you prepare for your shift. It does not replace facility policy, orientation, or clinical training. Always follow your facility’s policies and procedures. Do not write patient identifiers on this form; dispose of completed copies per facility policy.

Print this page (Ctrl+P / Cmd+P) to use the checklist on paper. Headers and navigation are hidden when printing.

Before you leave home

  • Uniform/scrubs clean and ready (per facility dress code)
  • ID badge and facility access credentials
  • Stethoscope, penlight, pens, and personal tools
  • Watch with a second hand or timing device
  • Comfortable, supportive shoes
  • Water bottle and snacks/meals for the shift
  • Required certifications/cards (BLS, ACLS, etc.)
  • Reviewed schedule and shift start/end times
  • Transportation and childcare/eldercare arranged
  • Adequate sleep before the shift

On arrival (before your first patient)

  • Checked in and received assignment/patient list
  • Reviewed assignment for acuity and special precautions
  • Located emergency equipment (crash cart, fire extinguishers, exits)
  • Identified charge nurse, preceptor, and team members
  • Reviewed unit-specific updates or policy changes
  • Set up your report sheet (blank, no patient info yet)
  • Confirmed break plan and coverage arrangements
  • Confirmed EHR/workstation login credentials

During handoff/report

  • Received handoff in facility’s required format (SBAR, I-PASS, etc.)
  • Took notes on your report sheet
  • Asked clarifying questions
  • Confirmed code status, allergies, isolation for each patient
  • Noted pending tests, procedures, and medications due
  • Identified time-critical tasks for the shift
  • Documented the handoff per facility policy (if required)

Mid-shift

  • Took scheduled breaks with coverage arranged
  • Kept report sheet updated as events occurred
  • Rechecked time-critical tasks and med due times
  • Escalated patient changes to provider/charge nurse
  • Documented care per facility policy and timing requirements

Before handoff to the next shift

  • Completed outstanding tasks or delegated them clearly
  • Updated report sheet with current status
  • Gave handoff in facility’s required format
  • Invited questions and confirmed read-backs where required
  • Documented the handoff per facility policy (if required)
  • Returned equipment and restocked supplies

End of shift

  • Signed out of all workstations/EHR
  • Collected personal belongings
  • Secured or destroyed paper notes per facility policy
  • Left work area tidy for the next shift
  • Clocked out per facility timekeeping policy

After the shift

  • Debriefed anything difficult (no patient details in public)
  • Hydrated and ate
  • Wrote down anything still on your mind (“brain dump”)
  • Planned rest and recovery before the next shift

AI Aids for Drafting Notes — Education Only

Never paste real patient information into an AI tool. No names, dates of birth, medical record numbers, room numbers, or dates of service. AI output is a draft only — a qualified clinician must review it before anything goes into a medical record. Follow your facility’s policy; some facilities prohibit AI for documentation entirely.

What AI can help with (fictional practice cases only)

  • Rewording a practice note for clarity and tone
  • Organizing a note into a standard format (e.g., SOAP: Subjective, Objective, Assessment, Plan)
  • Drafting patient-education handout language (clinician review required before use)
  • Creating practice scenarios to learn documentation structure

What AI should NOT do

  • Determine a diagnosis, treatment, or plan for a real patient
  • Replace clinical assessment or judgment
  • Generate a note you copy-paste directly into a real chart without review
  • Handle real patient data of any kind

Practice example (completely made up)

Practice input:
Practice patient reports feeling better today, less shortness of breath. Practice vitals: T 98.6, HR 88, RR 18, SpO2 96% on room air. Practice lung sounds clear.
AI rewrite aid (fictional):
The practice patient reports improved dyspnea today. Practice vital signs are stable: T 98.6°F, HR 88, RR 18, SpO2 96% on room air. Practice lung sounds are clear to auscultation. Assessment: improving pneumonia. Plan: continue current treatment plan; reassess in the morning.

None of this is about a real person. Do not document real patients this way.

Privacy quick check before typing into AI

  • Could this identify a real patient? If yes, stop.
  • Is this my facility’s approved tool? If unsure, don’t use it.
  • Am I copying this into a real chart? If yes, a clinician must review it first.

When in doubt, leave it out.
